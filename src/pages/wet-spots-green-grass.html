How do I tell a rainy-season low spot from a field that's surfacing?

Lehigh Acres lots get very wet in a stormy month. A corner that dries a day or two after the rest of the yard may just be poor grade. A strip that stays spongy, grows a much brighter green, or gives off a sewage odor after the neighborhood has dried is more suspicious, especially if it sits where you believe the field is. Indoor signs — gurgles, a slow toilet, a smell in the house — make the case stronger. Still, the yard alone is not a diagnosis. We have to open the tank to see whether it is simply full or whether water is coming back from the field.

What should I stop doing on that patch today?

Don't drive or set a dumpster on it. Don't spread more fill, sand, or sod to "hide" it. Don't keep running laundry as if nothing is wrong. Ease off water use and keep kids and pets off a smelly, wet area. If sewage is on the ground, treat it as contaminated. If water is also coming up inside, stop fixtures and use the backup steps. Stay off it with vehicles. Keep oil and wipes out of the drains. Don't aim irrigation at that strip all afternoon. The habit list is on drain field do's and don'ts. FAQ

Wet-spot and green-grass questions

The grass over one strip is much greener. Is the field done?

Not by itself. Extra-green grass can be a clue, especially with a soft spot or an odor. Call (239) 506-1163. We inspect before anyone talks about replacement.

Can I add fill and new sod over the wet area?

Don't. Covering it hides the symptom and can make the soil even less able to take water. Leave it open and call us.

Should I stop watering that part of the yard?

Yes. Don't add irrigation to a strip that is already wet. See also our page on pools and irrigation near a septic system.

Will you start with a pump-out?

Often that is the first useful step, so we can see whether the tank was full and whether water comes back from the field. A standard pump-out is $399.99 for a 1–3 bedroom home, confirmed on the call.
