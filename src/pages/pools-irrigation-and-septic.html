Pools and irrigation near a Lehigh Acres septic system

Don't place a pool, a patio, or a sprinkler pattern on top of the drain field, and don't empty a pool or spa into the yard over those lines. A large slug of water — especially chlorinated water — can overload soil that is already working hard in the rainy season. Where should a pool or a patio not go?

Not on the drain field, and not so close that equipment, a deck, or a future fence will sit on the lines or the lids. Rapid already tells builders not to pour a slab or park equipment on the field, and a pool is the same kind of load. We do not design pools and we do not pull pool permits. What we can do is help locate the tank and lids so you aren't guessing, and pump or inspect the system if the job or a closing needs it. Locating is quoted on the call; see tank locating and the builders and contractors page.

Can I drain the pool or the spa into the yard?

Not onto the drain field. A large volume of water can saturate the soil so it cannot take wastewater from the house. Chlorinated water is also a harsh load for the bacteria that treat effluent in that soil. Discharge has to go somewhere the field is not. This page is not a drainage permit guide; it is a request not to treat the septic field as a dump zone for pool water.

How should irrigation be aimed once the pool is in?

Don't soak the field all afternoon. The soil there already takes water from the house. Extra irrigation, plus Lehigh Acres' rainy season, is how a field starts to look perpetually wet even when the tank was recently pumped. Irrigation lines can also run through the same part of the yard as the lids; Rapid already warns not to dig blindly and to call 811 before any digging of your own. If a lid disappears after a landscaping job, don't start a row of test holes — use the missing-lid page and call us.

The yard is soft after the pool contractor left. What now?

Stay off the soft strip with vehicles. Ease off sprinklers and laundry. If there is an odor, or drains in the house have changed, call {{PHONE}}. We inspect first: empty the tank if that is the right step, look at the inlet and the outlet, and see whether water comes back from the field. FAQ

Pool and irrigation questions

Can Rapid mark the tank and field before we dig a pool?

We can locate lids and open the tank so you know where the system sits. We don't survey a field the way an engineer would. Call (239) 506-1163 before the excavator arrives and tell us a pool is planned.

Is it all right to backwash or empty the pool onto the drain field?

No. A large volume of water, and chlorinated water in particular, does not belong on the field. Send that water somewhere else.

Our sprinklers run on the same side of the yard as the tank. Is that a problem?

Don't keep soaking the field. Also know that irrigation lines may be near the lids, so don't dig there without calling 811. We can locate a buried lid; that work is quoted on the call.

Will a new pool change the pump-out price?

The published standard pump-out is still $399.99 for a 1–3 bedroom home, $449.99 for 4 bedrooms and $499.99 for 5 bedrooms, confirmed before we start. A tighter yard or a buried lid can change the total; we'll quote any extra work first.
