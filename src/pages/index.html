Septic pumping in three simple steps

For a routine residential pump-out, here's what to expect from first call to last hose.

  1. You call or request a quote

    Tell us where the property is, what's going on and when the tank was last pumped, if you know. Mention gates, pets or sprinkler lines so there are no surprises.

  2. We find, open and pump the tank

Lehigh Acres

Septic ownership in Lehigh Acres: what's different here

About 30% of Floridians rely on a septic system, roughly 2.6 million systems statewide.3 Lehigh Acres is a large, mostly residential community in eastern Lee County. Much of it was laid out decades ago as individual home lots, and many homes here have their own septic system instead of central sewer. If yours does, pumping is part of owning the house, just like servicing the AC.

{{i:calendar}} How often to pump

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency says household septic tanks are typically pumped every three to five years and inspected at least every three years.1 The right interval depends on tank size and how many people live there. A busy household or a garbage disposal shortens it.

{{i:rain}} Rainy season and saturated ground

Southwest Florida's summer rains can leave the ground around a drain field saturated for weeks, so slow drains and gurgling often show up after heavy rain. If the area is flooded, the EPA advises against pumping until the water goes down, because an empty tank in saturated ground can shift or float.2 We'll tell you if it makes sense to wait.

{{i:search}} Finding the tank

It's common here for the lid to be buried under sod, pavers or landscaping, especially on older homes or after a sale. Knowing roughly where the tank is saves time on the day. If you don't know, we offer septic tank locating.

{{i:truck}} Access for the truck

The pump truck parks in the street or driveway and runs a hose to the tank. Let us know about locked gates, fences, dogs or anything the hose has to cross.

Florida septic rules and permits

Since July 2021 the Florida Department of Environmental Protection has been responsible for the state's septic rules, but permits and inspections are still handled by each county's health department.3 In Lehigh Acres, that means the Florida Department of Health in Lee County. Routine pumping doesn't need a permit. Installing, replacing or repairing a system usually does.

FAQ

Septic pumping questions we hear most

How often should a septic tank be pumped in Florida?

Most household tanks need pumping every three to five years. That's the EPA's general guidance. A larger family, a smaller tank or heavy use of a garbage disposal means more often. If you don't know when it was last done, it's worth having it pumped so you have a starting point.

How much does septic tank pumping cost in Lehigh Acres?

It depends on the size of the tank, how easy it is to reach, whether the lid has to be found and dug up, and how long it's been since the last pump-out. Call with your address and anything you know about the tank, and we'll quote it for you. See what affects the cost of septic pumping.

What happens during a septic pump-out?

We locate and open the tank, run the vacuum hose from the truck, and remove the liquid, sludge and scum. With the tank empty we check the inlet and outlet, close it back up and tell you what we saw. Read the step-by-step septic pumping guide.

How long does septic pumping take?

A routine residential pump-out usually takes about an hour on site once the lid is open. It takes longer if the tank has to be located and dug out first, or if the tank is very full.

How do I know if my septic tank is full?

The usual signs are slow drains throughout the house, gurgling toilets, sewage odors, soggy ground over the tank or drain field, and backups in the lowest drains. The only way to know for sure is to open the tank and look.

Can heavy rain cause septic problems?

Yes. When the ground around the drain field is soaked, water from the tank has nowhere to go, so drains can slow down or back up even if the tank isn't especially full. If the yard is flooded, pumping may need to wait until the water goes down. See septic backups after heavy rain.

What are the warning signs of a septic backup?

Gurgling in one drain when another runs, several fixtures draining slowly at once, sewage smells, and water coming up in the lowest drain in the house (usually a shower or tub). If wastewater is already coming up, stop using water and call.

What should I do if my septic tank backs up?

Stop using water: no flushing, laundry, dishwasher or showers. Keep people and pets away from any sewage, and don't try to open the tank yourself. Then call us at {{PHONE}}. Our emergency septic service page has the full checklist.

What is the new septic law in Florida?

Florida's House Bill 1379 (2023) requires nitrogen-reducing systems instead of conventional septic systems for new systems on lots of one acre or less in certain water-quality "impacted" areas. It doesn't change how you maintain or pump an existing system. Whether a property is in one of those areas depends on its location. The Florida Department of Health in Lee County or the Florida DEP HB 1379 page can confirm.
