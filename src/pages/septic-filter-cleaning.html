How can I tell if my tank even has a filter?

Many older tanks on the Lehigh Acres grid have a baffle at the outlet and no cartridge filter at all. Newer tanks sometimes have a filter in the outlet side, under that lid, to catch solids before they leave for the drain field. A permit, an as-built, or a prior service note may mention it. If you have no paperwork, don't assume. The only reliable way is to have the outlet opened. Rapid can look at the inlet and the outlet once the tank is empty; tell us you want that outlet fitting checked so it isn't skipped if the first lid we find is only the inlet.

What does a clogged outlet filter feel like from inside the house?

Drains that used to be fine start to lag. A toilet gurgles. In a worse case, wastewater comes back up, which is the same way a full tank or a field that isn't taking water can present. Rapid's emergency page already lists a blocked outlet or filter as one cause of a backup. You cannot tell those apart from the bathroom. Stop adding water if sewage is coming up, and use the steps on what to do during a backup. Then call. We are open 24 hours.

Should I pull the filter out myself?

Only if you already know where the outlet lid is, can open it safely, and have been shown how that unit comes out. A filter yanked the wrong way can dump a wad of solids toward the field, and a lid you can't seat again is a hazard. If the access is buried, don't start digging trails across the yard; irrigation, electrical and gas lines can be in the same area, and you should call 811 before any digging of your own. Locating a buried lid is a service we already offer, quoted on the call, often handled on the same visit as a pump-out.

What should I ask Rapid to do on a regular pump-out?

Say you want the outlet side opened and, if a filter is there, looked at. We empty the tank — sludge and scum, not just the liquid — and, with it empty, we check the inlet and the outlet. What we can do with a filter on that visit is something to confirm when you call {{PHONE}}; it is not a priced add-on on this site and it is not a separate "filter-only" appointment we advertise. A standard pump-out for a 1–3 bedroom home is listed at $399.99, with $449.99 for 4 bedrooms and $499.99 for 5 bedrooms, confirmed before we start. If the outlet lid needs a riser so you can reach it later, that is also something to ask about; risers are work we do, quoted on the call, with no published riser fee.

If the filter is dirty, does that mean the drain field has failed?

Not automatically. A clog at the outlet can starve the field of flow or push water back toward the house while the soil itself is still usable. The opposite is also true: a clean filter will not fix a field that has stopped taking water. After the tank is empty we look at whether liquid keeps coming back from the field. That question belongs with a field that isn't draining, not with a filter page.
