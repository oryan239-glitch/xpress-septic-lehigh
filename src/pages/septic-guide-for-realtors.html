How should I confirm the listing is on septic, not FGUA sewer?

Lehigh Acres is mixed. Some streets have been connected to Florida Governmental Utility Authority sewer; many lots still treat wastewater on site. Before you write "septic" into remarks, look at the utility bill and ask the owner whether they have ever had the tank pumped. A sewer line item usually means the house is no longer on a tank. Guessing in the MLS helps no one at inspection time. If the owner is unsure, Rapid can take a look when we are on the lot.

What belongs in the property file before you go live?

A last service date, a receipt, a permit or as-built, and a note on where the lids were opened. Buyers who have only lived on city sewer will ask basic questions; you can point them to our first-time owner guide for Lehigh Acres rather than improvising a lecture in the driveway. If the tank has not been opened in years, or nobody can find the lid, schedule that work before a rush of showings, not the week of closing. Locating a buried lid is part of what we do; it can change the total on a pump-out, and we tell you before we start.

When should an agent order a pump-out or inspection?

As soon as a contract is serious enough to have a date, or sooner if you are prepping a listing that has no history. A septic tank inspection often includes pumping because an empty tank makes the walls, baffles, inlet and outlet easier to see. Tell us it is for a sale when you call {{PHONE}} so we can plan around access, pets, and lockboxes. Rapid does not publish a standalone inspection fee on this site; we'll confirm the visit on the call. A standard pump-out, when that's all that's needed, is $399.99 for a 1–3 bedroom home, $449.99 for 4 bedrooms and $499.99 for 5 bedrooms.

What should I tell buyers who have only lived on municipal sewer?

Keep it short: the tank has to be emptied on a schedule, wipes and grease do not go down the drain, and the drain field in the yard is not a parking pad. They should keep a record of the next service date. If they want more than a hallway explanation, share the first-time guide and the maintenance checklist. You do not need to diagnose a wet spot in the backyard yourself; that's a call to us or a reason to order the inspection.

What if a backup shows up during the option period?

That's an active problem, not a paperwork item. The occupants should stop running water and call. Rapid is open 24 hours. Walk them to what to do during a backup and to 24-hour emergency service. We quote emergency work on the call; there is no separate after-hours price list on this site. If the yard is saturated after a storm, the EPA advises waiting to pump a tank in a flooded yard because a tank can shift or float; we'll help you judge that when you call.
