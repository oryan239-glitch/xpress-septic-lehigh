What should I do in the next few minutes?

Shut off anything that adds water: washing machine, dishwasher, showers, ice maker. Don't keep trying the toilet. Keep children and pets away from any water on the floor. If you can, note whether one fixture is failing or several, and whether the yard is underwater from a storm. Then call {{PHONE}}. Don't wait on an online request while the backup is still happening. For how we respond once we're on the way, see 24-hour emergency septic service in Lehigh Acres.

How is a backup different from a single slow drain?

A lavatory that is merely sluggish, or a gurgle that started yesterday, may still be a scheduled pump-out. Water standing in a shower, a toilet that won't go down, or sewage on the floor is a now problem. If you aren't sure, call anyway and describe it. A blocked outlet or filter, a full tank, or a field that isn't taking water can all show up the same way from inside the house until the tank is opened.

What will Rapid do when the truck arrives?

We locate the lid — digging it out if we have to — and empty the tank. With it empty we check the inlet and the outlet, and whether water keeps coming back in. A full tank is the most common cause, and pumping often stops the indoor backup. If something else is going on, we tell you. Further repair is quoted after we see the system. A buried lid or extra work can change the total; we confirm that before we start. There is no separate published after-hours price list on this site.

What if this started during a flood or a long rain?

Tell us. If the yard is actually flooded, pumping may need to wait. The EPA warns that emptying a tank in waterlogged soil can cause it to shift or float. Once standing water is down, we can look at the tank and, if the field is still not taking water, at why the drain field isn't draining. For planning before the next storm, use the hurricane and rainy-season page.

After the water goes down, what should I change?

Get the last pump-out date on a calendar. Most household tanks follow the EPA's general three-to-five-year range, but a backup is a sign you should not wait for the far end of that range without a look. Keep wipes and grease out of the system. A standard, non-emergency pump-out is $399.99 for a 1–3 bedroom home, $449.99 for 4 bedrooms and $499.99 for 5 bedrooms. Those figures are for a routine visit, confirmed on the call, not an automatic emergency total.
