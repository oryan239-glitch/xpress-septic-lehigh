How do I tell if my Lehigh Acres house is still on a tank?

Not every lot in Lehigh Acres uses a septic system. The Florida Governmental Utility Authority (FGUA) provides sewer to part of the community. If your bill includes a sewer charge, the house may already be connected and this guide does not apply. If there is no sewer charge and you have a lid, a mound, or a cleanout in the yard, you are likely on a tank. When you aren't sure, call {{PHONE}} and describe the lot; we can help you sort that out when we come out.

Where does the water from the house go once it leaves the pipes?

Toilets, sinks, showers and laundry all drain to an underground tank. Heavier material settles. Lighter material, including kitchen grease, floats. The clearer liquid in the middle leaves the tank and soaks into a drain field in the yard. If the tank has a filter or a baffle at the outlet, that fitting is meant to keep more solids from riding out with the liquid. Rapid empties the tank with a vacuum truck and, with it empty, can look at the inlet and the outlet and tell you what we saw.

What paperwork should I look for in the first month?

Ask the seller, the closing file, or a prior landlord for the last pump-out date, any inspection notes, and a sketch or permit that shows where the tank sits. Lee County records can also help you find a buried lid. If nobody has a date, treat the system as overdue until it has been opened. A first visit is often a pump-out; if you are mid-purchase, ask about a septic tank inspection at the same time. We do not publish a separate inspection price; we'll confirm the visit when you call.

When should a new owner have the tank emptied?

The U.S. EPA's care guidance is that most household tanks are pumped every three to five years. That is a range, not a calendar that fits every Lehigh Acres house. More people in the home, a smaller tank, a garbage disposal, or a house that sat empty and then filled up quickly can all mean you should not wait for the far end of that range. If you have no records, schedule the first pump-out rather than guessing. Once the lid is open, a routine residential visit usually takes about an hour; it takes longer if we have to locate and dig the lid first.

What daily habits matter most on a Lehigh Acres lot?

Keep wipes, paper towels, feminine products and grease out of the drains. Thick "flushable" wipes still do not belong in a tank. Don't park or pour a slab over the drain field, and keep trees and big shrubs off the lines; grass is the better cover. After a heavy rain, a soggy yard is not automatically a failed field, but it is a reason to go easy on laundry and to call if drains inside the house slow down. For a punch-list you can hang in a rental or a guest bath, use the Lehigh Acres maintenance checklist.

Which early signs mean I should call, not wait?

Sewage odor in the yard or the house, gurgling, a toilet that is slow to clear, unusually lush or wet grass over the field, or water coming back up a drain. If wastewater is already in the house, stop running water and use the steps on what to do when a tank backs up. Rapid is open 24 hours. Is every Lehigh Acres house on a septic tank?

How often will I need pumping after that first visit?

The EPA's general range for most household tanks is every three to five years. Household size and tank size change that. We'll tell you what we saw after the first pump-out.
