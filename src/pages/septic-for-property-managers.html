What should sit in each property file besides the lease?

The last pump-out date, a note on where the lids were found, whether the lot is on a tank or on FGUA sewer, and who is authorized to approve a visit. Lehigh Acres is mixed: some managed homes are already on utility sewer, and those should not be on a pumping calendar at all. For tanks, the EPA's general range is every three to five years; a house that stays occupied, or turns over often, may need a shorter interval. If a file has no date, schedule the work at vacancy rather than waiting for a backup during a new lease.

How should an on-call manager handle a midnight backup?

The occupant should stop running water and the manager should call {{PHONE}}. Rapid is open 24 hours. Don't send a quote form while sewage is on the floor. Walk the occupant through the indoor backup steps if they need something in writing. We quote emergency work on the call. Have a way to get us into the yard — a lockbox, a gate code, a person with a key — so the truck isn't waiting in the street. After the visit, log what we found so the owner sees the date and any follow-up, such as a lid that should get a riser or a field that needs a look.

How do you want a turnover or a listing inspection scheduled?

Call with the address, the bedroom count, the access plan, and whether the visit is a pump-out, an inspection for a sale, or both. Tell us if the house will be vacant. A sale inspection often includes pumping so the inside of the tank can be seen. We don't publish a standalone inspection price; we'll confirm the visit on the call. A standard pump-out is $399.99 for a 1–3 bedroom home, $449.99 for 4 bedrooms and $499.99 for 5 bedrooms. If you also work with the owner as a small landlord operation, the landlord page is written for that shorter list of houses.

What can I hand to leasing staff or a new occupant?

The maintenance checklist is the short version: no wipes, no grease, stay off the field, call if something slows down. Occupants who have never lived with a tank can use the first-time owner guide. If a listing is going on the market, point the agent to the Realtor briefing and to buying or selling so the closing file has a service date, not a guess.

FAQ

Property manager questions

Can I keep several Lehigh Acres addresses on a regular pumping rotation?

Yes. Call (239) 506-1163 with the addresses and the last service dates you have. We don't publish a bundled "portfolio plan" price; each visit is confirmed from the standard pump-out list and whatever extra work that lot needs.

Who has to be on site when you arrive?

Someone has to provide access to the yard and, if the lid is inside a fence, a way in. A manager, an owner, or a tenant with permission can meet us. A lockbox or a gate code works when you tell us in advance.

Do you invoice the management company or the owner?

Tell us who should receive the bill when you book. We confirm the work and the price before we start. This page doesn't set a special management-company rate.

A tenant says the yard is wet after rain. Do I call for a replacement?

Not first. Saturated soil is common in the rainy season. Call us, cut back water use, and we'll inspect. We repair a field when that's still possible and only replace it when a repair will not hold.
