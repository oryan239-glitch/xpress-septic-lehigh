Does it matter whether the smell is indoors or only in the yard?

Yes, because it changes what we ask you on the phone. A smell only at a little-used sink or shower can be a dried trap — the water seal in the fixture — and running a bit of water there sometimes clears it. An odor in several rooms, or one that arrives with gurgling or a slow toilet, points at the tank or the lines. A smell that hangs over a strip of yard, especially if the grass there is wetter or greener than the rest, can be a lid that isn't seated or a field that is surfacing. You will not sort that out with a candle. Call {{PHONE}} and tell us where you notice it and whether any drains have changed.

What should I do if the odor is inside and getting worse?

Don't keep flushing "to push it through." If water is coming back up, treat it as a backup: stop using water, keep people and pets away from any spill, and follow the indoor backup steps. Sewage carries bacteria; the emergency page already advises gloves and keeping children out of the area. Rapid is open 24 hours. An online quote form can wait until the house is stable.

What if I only smell it when I walk the yard?

Look — don't dig — for a lid that has shifted, a low spot that stays soft, or a patch of extra-green grass. After a long rain in Lehigh Acres, a damp yard is common; a smell that stays after the rest of the lot has dried is more useful information. Don't cover the area with more soil or drive over it. We may need to find a buried lid before we can open the tank; that work is quoted on the call. Wet or bright-green ground over the field is covered on the wet-spots page.

What will a visit usually involve?

We open the tank, empty it if a pump-out is the right step, and look at the inlet and the outlet once it's empty. A full tank is a common source of odor and is often relieved by pumping. A blocked outlet or filter, or water coming back from the field, will show itself after the tank is open. We tell you what we saw. Repair, if something is broken, is quoted after that look, not from a packaged odor-service price. A standard residential pump-out, when that's all that's needed, is $399.99 for a 1–3 bedroom home, $449.99 for 4 bedrooms and $499.99 for 5 bedrooms, confirmed before we start.

FAQ

Sewage odor questions

Can a dry sink trap make a sewage smell?

Yes, in a little-used fixture. Run water in that sink or shower and see if the odor fades. If it is in more than one room, or the yard smells too, call (239) 506-1163.

Should I pour bleach to kill the smell?

No. A heavy pour of bleach will not empty a tank and can set back the bacteria the system needs. Ventilate the area and call us if the odor continues.

Is an odor after a storm always a failed drain field?

No. Wet ground is common in the rainy season. Call if the smell stays, if a soft spot doesn't dry, or if drains in the house have changed.

Are you open if this starts at night?

Yes. Rapid is open 24 hours, 7 days a week. Call (239) 506-1163. If sewage is coming up inside, stop running water first.
