What's involved

About drain field replacement

A drain field fails when the soil can no longer take in or treat wastewater from the tank. That often happens after solids escape a tank that hasn't been pumped, or after years of saturation in Southwest Florida's rainy season.

The first step is inspection. We pump the tank and check the system so we can tell whether the field can still be repaired. If a repair will do, that's what we recommend. We don't push replacements.

When the drain field has failed and can't be repaired, we handle the replacement ourselves. Drain field work in Florida may require a permit through the county health department.

If you're seeing soggy ground, odors or slow drains and aren't sure how far things have gone, start with drain field repair or call us and we'll walk through what we find.

FAQ

Drain Field Replacement FAQ

Do you replace drain fields, or only repair them?

We do both. We inspect first and repair when we can. If the field has failed and a repair won't hold, we replace it ourselves.

Will you try to repair my drain field first?

Yes. A full replacement isn't our first recommendation. If pumping or a repair will get the system working, we'll say so.
