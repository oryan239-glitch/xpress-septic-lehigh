Why the inlet and outlet sides of a Lehigh Acres tank both need to be emptied

A typical tank is not one open box: wastewater enters one side, and the clearer liquid leaves from the other. If only the easy liquid comes out, sludge and scum can stay behind and move toward the drain field. Rapid's pump-out is meant to remove all three layers — liquid, sludge and scum — and, once the tank is empty, we check the inlet and the outlet. Call {{PHONE}} to schedule.

What is the difference between the inlet side and the outlet side?

Waste from the house arrives at the inlet. Heavier solids drop out as sludge. Grease and other light material float as scum. The liquid in the middle moves toward the outlet, and from there to the drain field. Many tanks use two compartments, or at least two lids, so that settling happens before the water leaves. Some older Lehigh Acres tanks will not match a diagram you found online. The point is the same: material collects at both ends, not only under the lid that is easiest to find in the grass.

What does Rapid actually take out of the tank?

The pump-out is the same service people call "septic tank cleaning." A vacuum truck empties the tank. On our pumping page we describe breaking up the scum layer and packed sludge so everything comes out, not just the liquid on top. That matters because a tank that looks lower after a skim can still be carrying years of solids. With the tank empty, we look at the inlet and the outlet and whether water keeps flowing back in from the house or the field, then close the lids and tell you what we saw.

Why does leaving solids in one side matter on a Lehigh lot?

Once sludge and scum take up too much volume, solids ride out with the liquid and clog the drain field — the expensive part of the system. Lehigh Acres soil already works hard in the rainy season, when the water table is high. A tank that was only partly emptied can still send grit toward those lines. If the yard is already soft or unusually green over the field, that is a separate question we sort out after the tank is open; see when a field isn't taking water and wet spots and green grass.

What if we can only find one lid?

Many yards here hide one or both accesses under sod or fill. Tell us that when you book. Finding a missing lid is ordinary work, and locating a buried tank is a listed service we quote on the call, usually on the same visit. If you want the next pump-out to skip the shovel, ask about a riser; we install them, with no published riser price.

FAQ

Inlet and outlet pumping questions

Do you only take the water off the top of the tank?

No. Rapid's pump-out is described as removing the liquid, the sludge and the floating scum, and breaking up packed material so it comes out. We then check the inlet and the outlet while the tank is empty.

My tank might have only one lid. Can you still empty it properly?

Tell us what you can see. Some tanks have more than one access. If a lid is buried, we can locate it; that work is quoted on the call at (239) 506-1163.

Is this a different price than a regular pump-out?

The published standard pump-out is $399.99 for 1–3 bedrooms, $449.99 for 4 bedrooms and $499.99 for 5 bedrooms. We confirm it before we start. Extra work, such as locating a lid, is quoted separately.

If the outlet looks clear, is the field automatically fine?

Not always. After the tank is empty we look at whether water comes back from the field. A wet yard or a backup after pumping needs its own look, not just a glance at the outlet pipe.
