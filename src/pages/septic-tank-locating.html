Why it happens

Why so many septic lids are hard to find

Septic tank lids are usually left below ground. Over the years they get covered by sod, mulch, pavers, a new patio or just soil washing over them. If the house has changed hands, nobody may remember where the tank is, and many owners only go looking when something goes wrong.

Finding the tank matters for more than the next pump-out. You need to know where it is so you don't park on it, plant trees over it, or build over it.

Septic tank location records to check before you call

  • Your home inspection report from when you bought the house. It may include a septic section with a sketch.
  • Old pump-out receipts. Some companies note where the lid is.
  • The original septic permit. Septic permits in Florida, which often include a site sketch showing where the tank is, are issued by each county's health department. For Lehigh Acres, ask the Florida Department of Health in Lee County for your property's septic records. The Florida DEP Onsite Sewage Program explains how state permitting works.
  • The previous owner or neighbors. In neighborhoods built at the same time, tanks are often in similar spots.

Clues you can look for yourself

  • Find where the main sewer line leaves the house, often near a bathroom, sometimes with a cleanout pipe sticking up. The tank is usually in that direction.
  • Look for a rectangle of grass that dries out or stays greener than the rest of the lawn.
  • Check for a slight dip or rise in the ground, or a small round or square cover at ground level.

Please don't dig blindly around the yard. Irrigation lines, electrical and gas lines can be in the same area. Call 811 before any digging of your own.

How we find it

How we locate a buried septic tank

  1. Start with records

    Anything you have (permit sketch, inspection report, old receipt) narrows the search.

  2. Follow the plumbing

    We work out where the main line leaves the house and which direction it runs.

  3. Probe carefully

    We probe the soil along that line to find the edges and top of the tank.

  4. Dig only what's needed

    We keep the hole as small as we can: just enough to reach the lid.

  5. Open and pump

    Once it's open, the tank can be pumped the same visit.

  6. Show you where it is

    We point it out so you can mark it for next time.

FAQ

Septic tank locating FAQ

Can you find my septic tank without any records?

Usually, yes. Records make it faster, but following the plumbing from the house and probing along that line will find most tanks.

Do I have to find the tank before you come out?

No. Just tell us when you call that you don't know where it is, so we can plan enough time for the visit.

How much does it cost to locate a septic tank?

It depends on how much there is to go on. With a permit sketch or old receipt it's often quick. With no records and a lot of landscaping, it takes longer. When locating is part of a pump-out, it's usually handled on the same visit. Call and we'll quote it.

How deep is a septic tank lid?

It varies. Some lids sit just under the grass, others are a foot or more down, depending on how the system was installed and how much soil has built up since.

Will locating the tank tear up my yard?

We keep digging to the minimum needed to reach the lid, and put the soil and sod back when we're done.

Is locating the tank a separate visit from pumping?

Normally it's done in the same visit: find the tank, open it, pump it. If you only need it located, for a home sale for example, tell us when you call.
