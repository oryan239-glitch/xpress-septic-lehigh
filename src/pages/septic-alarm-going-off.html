What is the alarm actually trying to tell me?

Not every Lehigh Acres house has one. When a system does, the alarm is usually warning that a pump tank or a lift station is higher than it should be, or that a pump didn't run when it should have. It is not a decoration, and it is not always a "full septic tank" in the simple residential sense. Some properties have a separate pump basin that moves wastewater toward a field or a sewer connection. Rapid already offers lift station pumping as a quoted service. Tell us whether the box is on the house, on a post in the yard, or at a small basin away from the main tank.

What should I do in the house while it is sounding?

Cut back water: laundry, showers, the dishwasher. If fixtures are already backing up, stop using water and follow the backup steps. Don't keep silencing a breaker or unplugging a pump unless a qualified person has told you it's safe; this page is not an electrical guide. Don't start digging around the basin. Call {{PHONE}} and stay where you can describe the property and how long the alarm has been on.

What can Rapid do when we arrive?

That depends on what is installed. We can pump a tank or, when the job is a lift station, pump the station. We look at the system we find and tell you what we saw. We do not advertise alarm repairs, control-panel rebuilds, or a fixed "alarm call" fee on this site. If a standard residential pump-out is what the main tank needs, those published figures still apply: $399.99 for a 1–3 bedroom home, $449.99 for 4 bedrooms, $499.99 for 5 bedrooms, confirmed before we start. Lift station work and any extra troubleshooting are quoted on the call.

After the alarm is quiet, what should I put in the file?

Note the date, what we pumped, and where the basin and the lids are. If the lot also has a regular septic tank, keep that pump-out date current too. For everyday habits, use the checklist. FAQ

Septic and lift-station alarm questions

Can I just switch the alarm off and use the house?

Don't ignore it. Cut back water and call (239) 506-1163. The alarm is warning that part of the system is not keeping up.

Do you service lift stations as well as house tanks?

Yes. Lift station pumping is a listed Rapid service, quoted on the call. Tell us if the alarm is at a basin or a station, not only at the main tank.

Is there a published price to reset an alarm?

No. We don't list a standalone alarm-reset fee. We'll tell you on the call what the visit can include and confirm the price before we start any pumping.

The alarm started during a flood. Should you pump right now?

Call and describe the yard. If the ground is flooded, pumping a tank may need to wait; the EPA warns that a tank in waterlogged soil can shift or float.
