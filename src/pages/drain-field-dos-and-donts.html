Drain field do's and don'ts for Lehigh Acres homes

Keep cooking oil and wet wipes out of the drains, use septic-safe toilet paper, go easy on bleach, and treat the drain field as soil that has to keep working — not as a parking space or a dumping ground for pool water. What should never go down the kitchen sink or the toilet?

Cooking oil, fat and grease belong in a can, not the drain. In the tank they float as scum and can move toward the field or help clog an outlet. Wet wipes — including ones labeled flushable — belong in the trash; they do not break down the way toilet paper does. The same goes for paper towels and feminine products. A biodegradable, septic-safe roll of toilet paper is the everyday choice. If you want a shorter list for a rental bathroom, use the maintenance checklist.

Why do big pours of bleach or harsh cleaner matter?

The tank and the soil in the drain field rely on bacteria to break down waste. Large amounts of bleach or heavy disinfectant can set those organisms back. Ordinary household cleaning is not the same as emptying a gallon of cleaner down a drain. If drains have already slowed, adding more product will not fix a full tank. What can I plant, park, or build over the field?

Grass is the cover that belongs there. Trees and large shrubs send roots after the lines. Cars, trailers, a shed, or a new slab compact the soil so it cannot take water. Keep roof gutters and yard irrigation from soaking the same strip all day. If you are planning a pool or a patio, read pools, irrigation and the septic system before the excavator arrives. Builders on an active lot can also start with the contractors page.

How should laundry and extra fixtures fit into a Lehigh Acres week?

Spread washing through the week instead of running every load in one morning. A garbage disposal adds solids, so you may need the tank opened sooner; the same can be true if a water softener sends extra water into the system when it recharges. Those details are on disposals and softeners. None of this replaces the pump-out itself. Most household tanks follow the EPA's general three-to-five-year range; a busy house should not wait until the far end if you have no date.

Habits help a working field. They will not reopen a field that has already failed, and they will not empty a tank that is overdue. FAQ

Drain field habit questions

Can I flush wipes if the package says they are septic safe?

No. Wipes, even those labeled flushable, should go in the trash. Use septic-safe toilet paper instead. This is the same advice already on Rapid's pumping pages.

Is a little bleach in the laundry a problem?

Normal household use is not the same as pouring a large amount of bleach or disinfectant down a drain. Big pours can set back the bacteria the tank and the field need.

Can I park on the drain field during a party or a move?

Don't. Vehicles compact the soil. Use the driveway or the street, even for a short visit.
