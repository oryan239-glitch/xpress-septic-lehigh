How it works

What happens when your tank is pumped

For a routine residential pump-out, here's what to expect from first call to last hose.

  1. You call or send a request

    Tell us where the property is, what's going on and when the tank was last pumped, if you know.

  2. We find and open the tank

    We locate the lid, dig it out if it's buried, and open the tank. Tell us about gates, pets or sprinkler lines ahead of time.

  3. The tank is pumped

    The vacuum hose runs from the truck to the tank and removes the liquid, sludge and the floating scum layer.

  4. We look at the condition

    With the tank empty we can see things you can't see when it's full, such as the inlet and outlet and how fast water is coming back in.

  5. The area is left clean

    The lid goes back on, the hose is packed up and the yard is left the way we found it.

  6. You get our recommendations

    We tell you what we noticed and roughly when the tank should be pumped again.

Warning signs

Signs your septic tank may need pumping

None of these alone proves the tank is full, but two or more at once is a good reason to call.

  • Slow drains all over the houseOne slow sink is usually a clog. Every sink, tub and shower slowing down at once points to the septic system.
  • Sewage odorsA rotten-egg smell inside, or outside near the tank or drain field.
  • Gurgling plumbingToilets or drains that gurgle when other fixtures run, or when the washer drains.
  • Standing water or soggy grassWet spots or unusually green grass over the tank or drain field, especially in dry weather.
  • BackupsWastewater coming up in the lowest drains, like a shower or tub.
  • Overdue maintenanceNo pump-out in the last three to five years, or you just bought the home and have no records.

If sewage is already coming up into the house, stop running water and call us right away.

Septic ownership in Lehigh Acres: what's different here

About 30% of Floridians rely on a septic system, roughly 2.6 million systems statewide.3 Lehigh Acres is a large, mostly residential community in eastern Lee County. Much of it was laid out decades ago as individual home lots, and many homes here have their own septic system instead of central sewer. If yours does, pumping is part of owning the house, just like servicing the AC.

How often to pump

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency says household septic tanks are typically pumped every three to five years and inspected at least every three years.1 The right interval for your home depends on tank size and how many people live there. A busy household or a garbage disposal shortens it.

Rainy season and saturated ground

Southwest Florida's summer rains can leave the ground around a drain field saturated for weeks. A drain field that's sitting in wet soil can't absorb water as well, so slow drains and gurgling often show up after heavy rain. If the area is flooded, the EPA advises against pumping until the water goes down, because an empty tank in saturated ground can shift or float.2 We'll tell you if it makes sense to wait.

Finding the tank

It's common here for the lid to be buried under sod, pavers or landscaping, especially on older homes or after a sale. Knowing roughly where the tank is saves time on the day. If you don't know, we can locate it.

Access for the truck

The pump truck parks in the street or driveway and runs a hose to the tank. Let us know about locked gates, fences, dogs or anything the hose has to cross, so there are no surprises on the day.

Florida septic rules and permits

Since July 2021 the Florida Department of Environmental Protection has been responsible for the state's septic rules, but permits and inspections are still handled by each county's health department.3 In Lehigh Acres, that means the Florida Department of Health in Lee County. Routine pumping doesn't need a permit. Installing, replacing or repairing a system usually does.

FAQ

Septic pumping questions we hear most

How often should a septic tank be pumped in Florida?

Most household tanks need pumping every three to five years. That's the EPA's general guidance. A larger family, a smaller tank or heavy use of a garbage disposal means more often. If you don't know when it was last done, it's worth having it pumped so you have a starting point.

How much does septic tank pumping cost in Lehigh Acres?

It depends on the size of the tank, how easy it is to reach, whether the lid has to be found and dug up, and how long it's been since the last pump-out. Call with your address and anything you know about the tank, and we'll quote it for you. See what affects the price.

How long does septic pumping take?

A routine residential pump-out usually takes about an hour on site once the lid is open. It takes longer if the tank has to be located and dug out first, or if the tank is very full.

How do I know if my septic tank is full?

The usual signs are slow drains throughout the house, gurgling toilets, sewage odors, soggy ground over the tank or drain field, and backups in the lowest drains. The only way to know for sure is to open the tank and look.

Can you locate my septic tank?

Yes. Many lids in Lehigh Acres are buried under grass or landscaping. We use where the plumbing leaves the house, any records or permits you have, and careful probing to find the tank so it can be opened and pumped.

What should I do if my septic tank backs up?

Stop using water: no flushing, laundry, dishwasher or showers. Keep people and pets away from any sewage, and don't try to open the tank yourself. Then call us at (239) 506-1163.

Do I need to be home during the pump-out?

Not always. If we can get to the tank and you've told us about gates, pets and where the lid is, we can often do the job without you there. It does help to be around so we can walk you through what we found.

What is the new septic law in Florida?

Florida's House Bill 1379 (2023) requires nitrogen-reducing systems instead of conventional septic systems for new systems on lots of one acre or less in certain water-quality "impacted" areas. It doesn't change how you maintain or pump an existing system. Whether a property is in one of those areas depends on its location. The Florida Department of Health in Lee County or the Florida DEP HB 1379 page can confirm.
