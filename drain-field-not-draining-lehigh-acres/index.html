When a Lehigh Acres drain field is not taking water

A wet or smelly strip over the drain field is a reason to call, not a diagnosis by itself — a full tank, a blocked outlet, or a week of rain can look similar until the tank is opened. Rapid inspects first and repairs the field when that's still possible. Call (239) 506-1163.

What does a field that isn't taking water look like in this town?

On a Lehigh Acres lot you may see a patch that stays soft after the rest of the yard has dried, grass that is much greener in one strip, a sewage odor along the field, or drains inside the house that slow down after several loads of laundry. In the rainy season the water table is already high, so a wet yard after a storm is not automatically a failed field. It is a reason to ease up on water use and to call if the house itself starts to gurgle or back up.

What should I do before anyone digs up the yard?

Cut back laundry, showers and the dishwasher. Don't park on the soft ground. Don't keep adding fill or more pipe on your own. If wastewater is coming up inside the house, treat it as a backup: stop the water and follow the indoor backup steps. Rapid is open 24 hours. If the yard is actually flooded, pumping may need to wait. The EPA's flood guidance warns that emptying a tank in waterlogged soil can cause it to shift or float. Tell us what the yard looks like when you call.

How does Rapid tell a full tank from a field that has failed?

We find the lid, empty the tank, and look at the inlet and the outlet, and whether water keeps coming back in. A full tank is the most common cause of a backup, and pumping often relieves the house. If liquid keeps returning from the field, or the soil clearly cannot take water, we talk about drain field repair. We don't push a replacement when a repair will still hold. When a field has failed and can't be repaired, we replace it ourselves. That work is quoted after the inspection, not from a published package price. What habits keep a still-working field from getting worse?

Keep solids in the tank by pumping on a sensible schedule — the EPA's general range for most household tanks is every three to five years — and keep grease and wipes out of the drains. Don't drive or build on the field. Spread heavy water use instead of running every machine on the same afternoon. A short version of those habits is on the maintenance checklist. FAQ

Drain field drainage questions

Is a soggy Lehigh Acres yard after rain a failed drain field?

Not by itself. The rainy season saturates a lot of yards here. Call (239) 506-1163 if the soft spot stays, if there is an odor, or if drains in the house slow down. We inspect before anyone talks about replacement.

Will you try to repair the field before replacing it?

Yes. We inspect first and repair when we can. A full replacement isn't our first recommendation. If the field has failed and a repair won't hold, we replace it ourselves.

Should I pump the tank if the field looks wet?

Often the first useful step is to empty the tank and see whether water comes back from the field. If the yard is flooded, tell us; the EPA advises waiting to pump a tank in waterlogged soil.
